# Double Diamond

Explore the problem before selecting a solution. Diverge and converge on both problem framing and possible solutions; keep decisions grounded in evidence.

## Working rules

- Enter at the phase supported by existing evidence. Reuse supplied findings; do not restart discovery by default. Revisit earlier phases when evidence challenges the framing or solution.
- Scale effort to uncertainty and consequence. A small task may need only a short problem statement, contrasting options, and one next test. Do not require personas, maps, workshops, or four separate reports.
- Distinguish observations, interpretations, assumptions, and unknowns. Preserve source links or supplied evidence references. Never invent interviews, quotations, findings, or test results. Label hypothetical personas and simulated reactions; generated viewpoints are not independent stakeholder evidence.
- Ask the user when disputed framing, priorities, or commitments materially change direction. Otherwise state provisional assumptions and proceed within scope, without ceremonial approval at every phase.
- Default to analysis, test planning, and decision handoff. Obtain separate authorization for implementation or external research activities involving people or commitments.

## Workflow

### Discover: expand the problem space

Identify affected people, their context, the desired change, constraints, and available evidence. Consider alternative explanations for the request, especially when it names a solution already.

Identify consequential gaps and the smallest useful investigation. If evidence is insufficient, return a research question or proceed with an explicitly provisional framing; do not present desk research as direct user research.

### Define: select a working problem

Separate supported findings from assumptions. State whose problem matters, what outcome should improve, relevant constraints, and observable success criteria. Avoid embedding a preferred solution in the problem statement.

Check whether the framing is supported enough to explore solutions. Resolve material disagreements with the user; retain remaining uncertainty explicitly.

### Develop: expand and compare solutions

Generate meaningfully different approaches before evaluating them. Include a simpler, non-build, or no-change option when relevant. Compare against the working problem, evidence, constraints, feasibility, and risks rather than novelty alone.

Return a reasoned shortlist. Identify the assumption most likely to invalidate each leading option and a cheap way to test it. Select what deserves testing, further exploration, or rejection; do not invent numerical precision for subjective rankings.

### Deliver: test and decide

For the leading uncertainty, specify the question, method, evidence to collect, and success or failure criterion before prospective testing. When reviewing existing results, distinguish pre-existing criteria from retrospective judgments. Execute tests only within the authorized scope and available capabilities.

When results exist, compare them with the criterion, report limitations, and recommend proceeding, revising, returning to an earlier phase, or stopping. Negative results may challenge the problem framing, not just the solution. When follow-up evidence comes from a small sample, state its scope and treat it as hypothesis-generating; use it to select one bounded next check, not as grounds for a broad study or a representative claim.

Without results, provide the test plan and mark validation pending. A plausible concept, prototype, or test plan is not proof that the underlying assumption holds.

## Handoff

Return only what the next decision needs: the working problem, evidence and assumptions, shortlisted or chosen direction with reasons, unresolved risks, and next test or decision. State what remains unvalidated. Do not produce a full specification or implementation plan unless requested.

When delegating, preserve the worker's role boundary. An ideation worker can receive the problem frame, constraints, evidence, and uncertainties and return distinct options with a ranked shortlist. Keep ownership of research, validation, and the overall synthesis and decision handoff with the coordinating agent. If no separate worker is actually available or invoked, say so and label any options you generate yourself as provisional; never imply they came from a worker. Do not require delegation to use this skill.
